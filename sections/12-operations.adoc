[[cl-operations]]
== Operations and durability

The framework's guarantees survive contact with operations or they do
not exist. This clause states the operational invariants as normative
requirements; each is demonstrated by the reference operator tooling
(`unidpp-ops` and the pilot deployment, see the bibliography's
implementation references).

[[cl-ops-data]]
=== The deployment as data

A deployment comprises its configuration (the operator manifest), its
services' journals, and its seed assets. Services SHALL reconstruct
their state by journal replay on start; no service SHALL hold
authoritative state that is not journaled. Configuration SHALL be
externalized data — a deployment's identity, services, and policies
SHALL be expressible without code changes (the whitelabel and
sovereign profiles are configuration, not forks).

[[cl-ops-backup]]
=== Backup integrity

A backup of a deployment SHALL carry, for every member it contains,
an integrity value (SHA-256) in an accompanying manifest, together
with the UTC moment of the snapshot and a consistency point: the
transparency log's signed tree head at snapshot time, delimiting what
a restore replays up to.

[[cl-ops-restore]]
=== Restore safety

A restore SHALL verify every member's integrity value before
unpacking any file. A restore SHALL refuse to overwrite an existing
deployment unless explicitly instructed; when instructed, it SHALL
preserve the replaced state (set aside, never destroyed). Member
paths belonging to the backed-up deployment's own tenant SHALL be
rewritten into the restored deployment; members belonging to other
tenants SHALL be restored as archive material under their original
identities (a restore SHALL NOT collapse distinct tenants onto shared
paths).

Recovery to the deployment's original shape (disaster recovery)
SHALL follow the same rules: verified before unpacking, the replaced
state set aside and never destroyed, and every member restored at
its original path — no rewriting of any kind.

[[cl-ops-append-only]]
=== Append-only continuity

Service journals are append-only across the service lifecycle: a
service upgrade or restart SHALL NOT rewrite, reorder, or truncate
any journal. The journals captured by a pre-upgrade backup SHALL be
byte-prefixes of the corresponding post-upgrade journals. An operator
SHALL be able to demonstrate this property by rehearsal: record the
domain state, rebuild and restart the services under the new
binaries, and show the readings equal and the prefix property holds.

[[cl-ops-drill]]
=== Proven restore

A backup that has never been restored is a claim, not a capability.
An operator deployment SHALL demonstrate its restore path by
rehearsal — backup, restore into an isolated rehearsal target owned
by the rehearsal itself, verify checksums, confirm restored members
byte-identical, and validate the restored configuration — and SHALL
record the rehearsal's outcome as an artifact. The rehearsal SHALL
NOT touch production state.

[[cl-ops-bundle]]
=== Transfer artifacts

Where a deployment is transferred to another host (on-premise or
sovereign operation), the transfer artifact SHALL carry per-member
integrity metadata verifiable independently of its producer, and
SHALL NOT embed secret material: secrets travel as environment
references, resolved at the receiving deployment.

[[cl-ops-grid]]
=== The segment grid

Where the preceding clauses govern the OPERATOR's deployment, the
grid governs the SUBJECT's data: a device DPP is one layered stack
crossed with parallel, policy-defined sovereignty segments.

A **segment** is a custodian's partition of the record; its wire
form carries a sealed state commitment and the governing policy
reference — never plaintext (a segment type that cannot hold facts
is the SG-7 log discipline at the subject level). A **policy
object** is the segment's constitution — authority, roles, reveal
class (open, pairing-gated, origin-sealed, escrowed), suites,
window, version — signed by the segment authority; verification
resolves the key through the trust graph node declared as the
authority, and a valid signature from any other key does not
constitute the policy. Superseded and drifted policy versions SHALL
be detected at verification (graded, never silent).

The **commitment spine** is one root over every segment's state
commitment. It SHALL prove existence, currency and append-only
growth of each segment without opening any: an inclusion proof for
segment k contains only hashes pertaining to segment k, and verifying
it requires nothing from any other segment. Rewinds and rewrites
SHALL NOT be provable as growth.

[[cl-s13]]
=== The cross-border seam (S13)

Where the grid governs one subject's data, S13 governs the
system-to-system request across a border: a verifier requests a
segment view; the custodian's POLICY answers. The outcome SHALL be
one of four machine-reproducible forms — permit, permit-paired,
attestation offer, escalation — derived from the governing policy's
reveal class; denial SHALL be stated with its reason, never silent.
The response SHALL bind to the request digest and cite the governing
policy identifier and version.

A **sovereign attestation** substitutes for an origin-sealed class:
a statement ABOUT the segment's commitment (never its contents),
claiming conformity, commitment-hash or freshness as of a stated
moment, signed by the sovereign attestation service. High-stakes
claims SHALL carry an M-of-K quorum co-signature. A verifier SHALL
accept such an attestation under its own anchors, and the resulting
verdict SHALL grade the evidence attested-by-authority — beside
verified-direct for opened evidence — naming the governing policy.

Every cross-border assurance flow SHALL work from exchanged signed
objects alone: the custodian can hand the verifier a **dossier** —
the signed policies, the signed spine with inclusion proofs, the
sovereign attestations, the journaled S13 exchange, one document —
and the verifier SHALL be able to derive the complete foreign
verdict offline, under its own anchors and acceptance policy, with
zero calls to the custodian's or any foreign synchronous system.

The **coverage report** is a first-class object: per data class, the
element set reference, the evidence kind (verified-direct /
attested / explicitly-unavailable), the governing policy identifier
and version, the reading, and the freshness. Direct and substituted
verification runs SHALL render their reports through the same
pipeline, and every report SHALL name the governing policies of the
classes it covers. Unavailability SHALL be stated as an entry, never
left silent.

Offers and refusals SHALL be non-repudiable: the custodian executes
the policy, but the policy AUTHORITY answers for it — the offer or
refusal carries the authority's counter-signature over the answer
and its place in the per-subject sequence, so a dispute is decided
from the record alone. A forged offer SHALL fail the authority's
signature; a replayed offer SHALL fail its sequence.

Acceptance is the RECEIVING profile's decision, never automatic: the
verifier's profile declares the attestation services it anchors, the
claim classes it accepts, the quorum strength it demands, the
freshness it requires, and the truth mode it tolerates per element
(only-opened evidence; attested evidence admissible; explicit
unavailability tolerable). The grade an attestation earns is the
minimum of what it proves and what the receiving profile accepts:
the same evidence under two profiles yields two different, both
correct, verdicts.
